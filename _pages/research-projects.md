### Working Papers

<ul class="research-entry-list">
  <li class="research-entry">
    <div class="research-entry__title"><em>When Do Anthropomorphic Emojis Enhance Information Sharing? A Visual-Linguistic Synergy Perspective</em></div>
    <div class="research-entry__authors"><strong>Yunzhou Zhong</strong>, <a href="https://ef.hksyu.edu/about-us/staff-list/detail/lee-tin-yun">Philip Tin Yun Lee</a>, <a href="https://scholar.xjtlu.edu.cn/en/persons/BingjieDeng/">Bingjie Deng</a></div>
    <div class="research-entry__meta">Invited for resubmission</div>
    <div class="research-entry__actions">
      <button type="button" class="research-entry__button" data-abstract-toggle aria-expanded="false" aria-controls="abstract-anthropomorphic-emojis">Abstract</button>
    </div>
    <div class="research-entry__abstract" id="abstract-anthropomorphic-emojis">
      <p>For organizations communicating public-interest information on social media, factual or psychologically distant content often fails to circulate. While prior research has examined what to communicate and whom to reach, the effect of micro-level visual cues such as emojis has been reported inconsistently, with the same cue eliciting divergent responses across linguistic and relational contexts that prior work has treated as separate contingencies. This paper develops and tests a construct of visual-linguistic synergy: the joint, supra-additive informational signal produced when an anthropomorphic visual cue co-occurs with a congruent linguistic cue within a single social-media post. By combining Social Information Processing theory with the Emotion as Social Information framework, we propose that anthropomorphic emojis enhance sharing not unconditionally but only when the linguistic context is congruent, operating through parallel pathways of perceived intimacy and alertness. A large-scale analysis of 742 organizational tweets on X reveals a crossover pattern: motivational language alone tends to suppress sharing (a non-significant negative association), but this pattern reverses when paired with an anthropomorphic emoji, while anthropomorphic emojis boost reposting only with personalized or motivational language. Two between-subjects experiments, with the linguistic context held supportive, provide causal evidence for the emoji effect, and a parallel-mediation test shows that the alertness route is robustly supported while the intimacy route is weaker and conditional, pointing toward a refinement of the dual-pathway model. The visual-linguistic synergy construct contributes to the information systems discourse on micro-level content design and offers feature-level guidance for platform designers, organizational communicators, and content policy.</p>
    </div>
  </li>
  <li class="research-entry">
    <div class="research-entry__title"><em>Too Neat to Be True? AI-Generated Review Summary Discrepancies and Consumer Response</em></div>
    <div class="research-entry__authors"><a href="https://siminli.com/">Simin Li</a>, <a href="https://jidingz.github.io/">Jiding Zhang</a>, <strong>Yunzhou Zhong</strong>{% include alphabetical-order-marker.html %}</div>
    <div class="research-entry__meta">Working paper</div>
    <div class="research-entry__actions">
      <button type="button" class="research-entry__button" data-abstract-toggle aria-expanded="false" aria-controls="abstract-ai-review-summaries">Abstract</button>
    </div>
    <div class="research-entry__abstract" id="abstract-ai-review-summaries">
      <p>Platforms increasingly display AI-generated review summaries (AIGS) alongside the user-posted reviews. Because these summaries are produced through an opaque process of selecting, weighting, and compressing review content, they may diverge from the recent human-review record. We examine how such discrepancies relate to consumer demand and whether their consequences depend on the direction of the discrepancy and the attribute on which it occurs. We collect Tripadvisor hotel pages across 31 cities during 2025 and pair AI-generated summaries with recent human reviews to construct hotel-period measures of AIGS–review discrepancy. Using imputed hotel demand as the outcome, we find that, within hotels, larger discrepancies are associated with lower demand. This negative relationship is concentrated in periods when the AIGS portrays a hotel more favorably than recent human reviews. The association also varies across attributes: overstatement is especially detrimental when it concerns information that consumers can readily verify before booking. Attribute-level estimates are largest for location, which prospective guests can independently assess using maps and other external information, and smaller for experience attributes that are difficult to evaluate before a stay. Summary-review alignment is therefore an operational diagnostic that platforms and hotels can monitor, recording the magnitude of a discrepancy together with its direction and the attributes it falls on. Our work carries important implications for both hotels and the platform. Depending on a hotel’s competitive strengths, the optimal strategy for displaying AI-generated review summaries may differ. These findings also offer guidance to platforms on how to design and implement AI-based review summarization across service providers they host.</p>
    </div>
  </li>
