<section class="home-intro-card" markdown="1">
I am a master’s student in [Behavioral and Decision Sciences](https://www.lps.upenn.edu/degree-programs/mbds) at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://www.upenn.edu/), where I am fortunate to conduct research under the supervision of [Prof. Lorin Hitt](https://oid.wharton.upenn.edu/profile/lhitt/) at the [Wharton School](https://www.wharton.upenn.edu/). I hold a Bachelor of Science with Honours from the [University of Liverpool](https://www.liverpool.ac.uk/) and a Bachelor of Management from [Xi’an Jiaotong-Liverpool University (XJTLU)](https://www.xjtlu.edu.cn/en), both earned through a [dual-degree program in Information Management and Information Systems](https://www.xjtlu.edu.cn/en/study/undergraduate/information-management-and-information-systems).

My research is in the economics of information systems. I study digital platforms and emerging technologies, especially artificial intelligence, and how the information and algorithms they introduce affect consumers, workers, firms, and regulators: what each can observe and do, how markets respond, who captures the value platforms create, and who bears the costs. This work currently spans two streams: how AI-generated content, online word of mouth, and social media shape what consumers learn, buy, and share; and how algorithms that set prices, and that decide who can work and when, shape firm strategy and competition, workers’ earnings, and platform regulation. Methodologically, I pair economic models with evidence from platform data, natural experiments, and field and laboratory experiments, using econometric methods, text analysis, and machine learning.
